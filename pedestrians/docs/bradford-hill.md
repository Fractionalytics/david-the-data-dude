# Bradford Hill scoring for the meth explanation (proposed 2026-11-09, for David to confirm)

**Source, verified 2026-11-09** from a reprint of the original (aliquote.org/pub/Hill_1965.pdf; PMC returned a CAPTCHA, not bypassed): Hill, A. B. (1965), "The Environment and Disease: Association or Causation?", *Proceedings of the Royal Society of Medicine* 58(5): 295–300. Hill lists nine "viewpoints" (his word; "criteria" is the later popular name), in this order: strength, consistency, specificity, temporality, biological gradient, plausibility, coherence, experiment, analogy. His caveat: none "can bring indisputable evidence for or against the cause-and-effect hypothesis"; they help judgment.

| Viewpoint | Proposed mark | Why | Card |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strength | none | Associations are modest: r = 0.35 across 38 states (0.65 without WV); meth users roughly 1.3–1.5× over-represented among homeless pedestrians killed (two states, two methods) | — |
| Consistency | ✓ | Two independent sources agree: victims' own toxicology (FARS) and state overdose deaths (CDC); the toxicology rise also holds in the four states with steady testing | 30, 34 |
| Specificity | none | Meth is tied to many harms, and pedestrian deaths have many causes | — |
| Temporality | ✓ | Stimulant share flat 2007–2014, rising from 2015, with the steepest rise in deaths (4,910 in 2014 → 6,080 in 2016) | 40–42 |
| Biological gradient | ✓ | States with bigger meth rises had bigger pedestrian rises (a state-level gradient, not individual dose) | 34 |
| Plausibility | ✓ | Wakefulness, impaired judgment (NIDA); crash phase of 1–3 days (SOFT) | 46 |
| Coherence | none (arguable) | The victim profile (adult men, night, poor areas) fits what's known about meth use, but we didn't source meth-use demographics | — |
| Experiment | ½ | Meth overdoses fell in 2024–2025 and pedestrian deaths fell too, but so did other road deaths; holding so far, not decisive | 48–51 |
| Analogy | none (could be ✓) | Meth raises crash risk for drivers (Elvik 2013; Drummer 2020). True, but the Reel doesn't show it | — |

Leaving four boxes empty is deliberate: it matches card 53 ("it's not perfect") and shows the scoring isn't stacked.
