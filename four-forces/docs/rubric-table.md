# The rubric: four forces × three lanes

Saved 2026-10-16. Research notes for David, not on-screen text. Data from OEWS (May 2019, 2022, 2025), verified; see `data/processed/takeaway_numbers.csv` and `exposure_ledger_summary.csv`.

The four forces are brakes on AI's effect on a job. The lanes are what happens depending on how many brakes a job has.

| Force (what it asks) | **Fast lane:** customer service, office & admin | **Slow lane:** radiology, law | **Third lane:** hands-on, in-person work |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Task vs. job:** is the automatable task most of the job, or one slice? | Most of the job: answering, routing, typing | One slice: reading images was 36% of a radiologist's time (VALUE study, 2012); drafting is one part of a paralegal's work | Almost none: the core task is physical and unpredictable (cleaning, lifting, repairing, caring for people) |
| **2. Gatekeepers:** regulation, liability, licensing: who has to sign? | Light: no license, low liability | Heavy: FDA, malpractice, the bar; a human must sign | Mostly light, but it doesn't matter: little for today's AI to take over |
| **3. Demand:** does cheaper work create more demand? | No: people don't call support more because it got cheaper (Hinton: "not so elastic") | Yes: cheaper scans mean more scans (Jevons; Hinton: "health care is elastic") | Mixed: driven by aging and construction, not AI |
| **4. Checking:** is the AI's work cheap to check, or do mistakes surface late? | Cheap: a bad answer shows up right away | Expensive: a missed diagnosis or bad filing surfaces late, at high cost | Mostly irrelevant: AI can't do the task yet; robots are the long-run question |
| **Data, 2022–25** | Customer service **−9.9%** (was −1.3%, 2019–22); office & admin **−4.9%** (was already −4.4%) | Paralegals **+13.8%**; radiologists grew (Radiology Reel) | ~27M low-exposure jobs, **+3.0%**; hand packers −14% (warehouse automation) |
| **Benchmark** | All jobs outside office & admin: **+6.6%** (+1.5% in 2019–22) | | |

**Logic:**
- **Fast lane:** none of the brakes apply.
- **Slow lane:** most of them do.
- **Third lane:** force 1 alone settles it, because AI has almost nothing to do there.

**Caveats:**
- Hinton's "elastic" quotes come from a machine transcript; check them against the video.
- The third lane's +3.0% comes from the balanced exposure panel, not the official-total benchmark.
