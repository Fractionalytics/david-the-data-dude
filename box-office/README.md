# Comedy Box Office

Why don't we see live-action comedies in theaters anymore? In June 2023, *The Ringer*'s Miles Surrey laid out why the theatrical comedy was fading. This project checks back in three years later, point by point, with 26 years of US box office data.

This folder is the behind-the-scenes companion to David the Data Dude's Instagram Reel.

## What we found

- **Comedies were a $3B-plus business, then weren't.** Live-action comedies averaged about $3.4B a year at the US box office from 2000 through 2009 (*The Hangover*'s year), in today's dollars. In 2025 they made $543M: down 84%.
- **Big original comedies are rare.** Since June 2023, only *Barbie* and *IF* are original live-action comedies that passed $100M. The last $100M rom-com was *Crazy Rich Asians* (2018).

Surrey's points, three years later:

| Surrey's point | Since his column |
|---|---|
| Streamers took the stars | Mixed. Adam Sandler stayed at Netflix, and *Happy Gilmore 2* (July 2025) had the biggest single week for a movie in Nielsen's streaming top 10: 2.893B minutes, in a 3-day week. But new stars broke out in theaters: *Anyone But You* (Dec 2023) made about $96M in today's dollars. |
| Audiences expect comedy at home | Early sign of change. The median wait before you can rent or buy a new release fell from 81 days (2016–19) to 32 (2023–25), then ticked up to 38 for films opening January–June 2026, after Universal dropped its 17-day policy (March 2026) and Paramount pledged 45 days (April 2026). |
| Comedy doesn't travel | From 2000 to 2019 he was right: comedies earned about 12 percentage points less of their box office overseas than similar-budget films. Since his column the gap is about 3 points, but see the correction below. |
| Studios want IP | Comedy survives inside franchises: 3 of the 5 $100M comedies since June 2023 are franchise films, and action and horror comedies went from 2.6 a year (2000–19) to 6.7 a year (2023–25). |
| His prescription: more mid-budget comedies | Some cheap comedies paid off: *Freakier Friday*, *One of Them Days* and *The Monkey* each cost $42M or less and grossed at least 3.6 times their budget worldwide. Expensive originals like *Fly Me to the Moon* flopped. |

## Definitions

- **Comedy:** a film whose genre is Comedy, Black Comedy or Romantic Comedy in The Numbers' labels, live action (hybrids with CGI co-stars, like *IF*, count; fully animated films don't). **Original** means not a sequel, spin-off or remake; the first film of a franchise counts as original.
- **Theatrical release:** reached 2,000 or more US theaters at any point in its run.
- **Box office:** each film's first 26 weeks in US/Canada theaters, adjusted to August 2026 dollars with the BLS consumer price index (CPI-U), summed by the year it opened. 2020 is left out (theaters were closed).
- **Overseas gap:** the share of a film's lifetime gross earned outside the US and Canada, compared with films of similar budget, franchise status and era (a regression).
- **Wait to rent or buy:** days from a film's first chart week to its first at-home release, usually digital rent-or-buy. Subscription streaming comes later.
- **Nielsen:** minutes watched on US TV sets in Nielsen's weekly streaming top 10.
- **Budgets** are published estimates; **multiples** are gross divided by budget, not profit.

## Corrections

Found in independent spot checks (Wikipedia, the BLS, studio press releases) run after the Reel was filmed:

| Where | The Reel shows | Corrected or clarified |
|---|---|---|
| Overseas chart | Since Surrey's column the gap is −2 points | **−3 points** (−2.6). *The Naked Gun* (2025) and *Haunted Mansion* (2023) had been counted as originals; fixing that moved the estimate. |
| "That gap has nearly closed" | The gap has nearly closed | True across all wide releases, but the narrowing comes from specialty-label comedies (*Poor Things*, *The Roses*, *Next Goal Wins*, *Materialists*). Major-studio comedies still trail similar films by about 9 points. |
| Budget multiples | *The Monkey* 5.8× its budget | About **6.3×**: our overseas figure for it was incomplete. |
| "Down 84%" | 84% | 84% using one consistent set of genre labels. One 2025 film labeled a comedy, *Companion*, is a thriller by most accounts; without it the drop is 85%. |

## Why there's no data here

Unlike our other projects, this folder has no datasets, tables or charts. Our box office figures come from The Numbers, whose [Terms of Service](https://www.the-numbers.com/terms-of-service) don't allow their data to be republished, in whole or in part. The figures above are the handful the Reel itself reports.
